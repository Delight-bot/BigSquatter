# Squat depth

## What counts as a full rep
The app counts a rep when the knee angle closes to about 100 degrees, which is roughly thighs parallel to the floor, and then opens back to standing. Depth is reported as a percentage of that target, so 100% means the rep reached parallel.

## Reps that stop short
Shallow reps are the most common reason a set feels easy but the count is low. Usual causes are rushing, fear of losing balance, and tight ankles. Slow the descent to a two-second count, sit the hips back as if reaching for a chair, and keep the chest up. Holding a light counterweight at arm's length in front of you makes it easier to sit deeper without tipping backwards.

## Improving depth over time
Depth improves with practice more than with stretching alone. A few slow bodyweight squats to a box or chair at the depth you want, before the main set, teach the position. Ankle mobility work, such as knee-to-wall rocks, helps people whose heels lift as they go down.
